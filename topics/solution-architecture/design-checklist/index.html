Solution Architecture Design Checklist

Questions to work through before and during solution design: business context, constraints, quality attributes, data, integration and operations, then decisions, boundaries, risk, validation and evolution.


Solution Architecture Design Checklist.md

System Design Question Bank

TL;DR

Part 1 is asked before drawing any boxes: it turns vague requirements into numbers, priorities and constraints, and each answer settles a specific design decision. Part 2 reviews a candidate design against those answers. Start with the Core 12.

How to use
  • Interviews: ask the Core 12 in the first 5–10 minutes, then state assumptions out loud for anything left unanswered.
  • Real projects: copy the and the sections you need into the project note, record the answers as given, and turn each significant answer into an ADR or a fitness function.
  • Push for numbers. "Fast" becomes a p99 in ms; "highly available" becomes a number of nines and a downtime budget.
  • "It depends" is a trade-off to record, not a gap to paper over. See Trade-off Analysis.
  • IDs are stable (B3, R2, A1…), so project notes and ADRs can cite the question they answer.

Core 12

  1. What problem does this solve, for whom, and how is it handled today?
  2. What are the 3–5 must-have use cases for the first release? What is explicitly out of scope?
  3. How many users (total, daily active, peak concurrent), and where are they?
  4. What is peak load (requests/sec, messages/sec), and what is the read:write ratio?
  5. How much data is there on day one, and how fast does it grow?
  6. Which three architecture characteristics matter most, and which are we willing to sacrifice?
  7. What is the availability target, and what does an hour of downtime cost?
  8. What latency do users need (p95/p99), and for which operations?
  9. Where is strong consistency required, and where is eventual consistency acceptable?
  10. What data is sensitive, and which regulations apply?
  11. What do we integrate with, and what happens when those dependencies fail?
  12. What are the fixed constraints: deadline, budget, team skills, mandated platforms?

Part 1 · Discovery (before design)

1. Business context

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
B1What business problem does this solve, what is the measurable outcome (revenue, cost, risk, compliance), and how does it support current strategy or OKRs?Success criteria; which characteristics to prioritize
B2Who are the stakeholders, who has the final say on scope and trade-offs, and who can veto?Decision rights; ADR approvers
B3What does the current process or system do, what's wrong with it, and what are users' biggest pain points today?Migration strategy; behavior that must not regress
B4What is the deadline, and what drives it (contract, regulation, market)?Build vs buy; scope cuts; how ambitious the architecture can be
B5What is the expected lifespan: throwaway, tactical (1–2 years) or strategic (10+)?Investment in evolvability vs time to market
B6What is the growth plan over 1, 3 and 5 years (markets, products, acquisitions)?Scalability headroom; extension points
B7What happens to the business if the system is down, slow, or wrong?Availability, latency, consistency and durability targets
B8Is this capability a differentiator or a commodity?Build vs buy vs SaaS
B9Why now? What happens if we don't build this, or build it six months later?Urgency; scope of the first release; build vs buy

2. Users and usage patterns

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
U1Who are the user types: customers, staff, partners, other systems?Authentication models; BFFs; API surface
U2How many users: total, daily active, peak concurrent?Capacity planning
U3Where are users located, and on what devices and networks?Regions; CDN and edge; offline or mobile support
U4What does traffic look like across a day, week, and year? Any flash events (sales, month-end, campaigns)?Elasticity; autoscaling; queue-based load leveling
U5Which operations are most frequent, and which are most important? (They often differ.)Where to optimize and cache
U6Do users need real-time updates (live dashboards, notifications)?SignalR/WebSockets, SSE, or polling
U7Are localization, multiple time zones or currencies, or accessibility standards required?UI and data model
U8Is the system multi-tenant? How isolated must tenants be, and do any need dedicated resources?Tenancy model (shared DB, schema per tenant, DB per tenant)
U9How technically skilled are the users, and how much training or support will they get?UX complexity; guided vs self-service workflows; support load

3. Functional scope

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
F1What are the core use cases, in priority order? What's MVP (must-have) and what's later (nice-to-have)?Scope; release plan
F2What is explicitly out of scope?Prevents silent scope creep
F3What are the core domain entities and the business rules that govern them?Domain model; bounded contexts; service boundaries
F4Which workflows span several steps or systems, and what happens if one fails halfway?Sagas; orchestration vs choreography; compensation
F5Which operations must complete while the user waits, and which can run in the background?Async processing; queues; workers
F6What reporting, search, or analytics is needed? Who uses it, and how fresh must it be?Read models; CQRS; reporting store; search index
F7Are there batch, scheduled, or long-running jobs?Worker services; job scheduling
F8What must business users be able to change without a deployment?Rules engine; feature flags; admin UI

4. Scale and capacity

Get numbers here, even rough ones. Back-of-envelope estimates come from these answers.

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
S1What are average and peak requests per second? What's the peak-to-average ratio?Instance sizing; autoscaling policy
S2What is the read:write ratio?Caching; read replicas; CQRS
S3How much data at launch, how much growth per month, and kept for how long?Storage choice; partitioning; archiving
S4How big are typical and largest payloads (records, files, media)?Blob storage; streaming; upload strategy
S5Are there hot spots (a huge tenant, a popular product, a celebrity account)?Partition/shard key; hot-partition mitigation
S6How many messages or events per second, and how large?Broker choice; partition count
S7How fast must capacity grow: minutes (autoscale) or seconds (pre-warmed)?Elasticity strategy
S8What headroom is needed in three years: 2×, 10×, 100×?Whether horizontal scale is designed in now or later

See Scalability and Caching Patterns.

5. Architecture characteristics

See Architecture Characteristics. Aim for the least worst architecture: you can't maximize everything.

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
Q1What are the top three characteristics, and which can we trade away?Architecture style; see Architecture Styles Compared
Q2What is the availability target? (99.9% ≈ 8.8 h/year down; 99.99% ≈ 53 min/year.) Are maintenance windows allowed?Redundancy; deployment strategy; cost
Q3What are the latency targets at p95/p99 for each key operation?Caching; data locality; sync vs async
Q4Which data must be strongly consistent, which can be eventually consistent, and how stale is acceptable?Transaction boundaries; replication; CAP Theorem trade-offs
Q5What are the RPO (data loss tolerated) and RTO (time to recover)?Backups; replication; DR design
Q6How often must we release, and is zero-downtime deployment required?CI/CD; blue-green or canary; DB migration style
Q7How many teams change this system, and how independently must they ship?Modularity; service granularity
Q8Must it be cloud-portable or run on-premises?Use of managed services; abstraction layers
Q9Do some characteristics apply to only part of the system?Architecture quanta; separate deployment units

6. Data

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
D1What are the main entities, which system is the source of truth for each, and who owns or stewards that data?Data ownership; service boundaries
D2What are the access patterns: by key, range, full text, relationships, aggregations?Database type; indexing
D3Which operations need ACID transactions, and do any span entities or services?Transaction boundaries; see Atomic vs Eventual Transactions
D4How long is data retained, and must it be deletable on request?Archiving; purge jobs; crypto-shredding
D5Is history or audit needed (who changed what, when; point-in-time views)?Temporal tables; event sourcing; audit log
D6What existing data must be migrated, and how clean is it?Migration and cutover plan
D7Who else needs this data (reports, other systems, a warehouse)?CDC; domain events; outbox; read replicas
D8Are there data residency requirements?Region pinning; per-region deployments
D9How often will the schema change?Migration tooling; schema versioning; expand/contract
D10Which data is critical (must never be lost or wrong), and which is derived (can be rebuilt)?Backup and replication scope; what can live only in caches or read models

7. Integration and interfaces

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
I1Which upstream and downstream systems and existing processes do we integrate with, and who owns them?Context map; dependency risk
I2For each: synchronous or asynchronous, and over what (REST, gRPC, messaging, files)?Communication style; coupling
I3What are their SLAs, rate limits, and failure behavior?Circuit breaker; retry; bulkhead; cache fallback
I4Who consumes our APIs: internal teams, partners, the public?API gateway; documentation; throttling
I5How are contracts versioned, and how are breaking changes communicated?Versioning strategy; consumer-driven contract tests
I6Can messages arrive twice or out of order? Must operations be idempotent?Idempotency keys; deduplication; ordered partitions
I7Must a DB write and an event publish succeed together?Transactional outbox
I8Are there legacy or file-based integrations?Anti-corruption layer; adapters

See Distributed Systems Pattern Catalog.

8. Security and compliance

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
X1Who authenticates, and how (OIDC/SSO, API keys, mTLS)? Is there an existing identity provider?Identity integration
X2What is the authorization model: roles, permissions, resource-level, attribute-based, tenant-scoped?Policy design; where checks are enforced
X3How is the data classified: PII, PHI, payment card data, secrets?Encryption; masking; access controls
X4Which regulations or standards apply (GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, SOC 2, SOX), and do they dictate data handling or residency?Controls; hosting; audit; see Data Compliance Standards Compared
X5What encryption is required at rest and in transit? Customer-managed keys?Key management
X6What audit trail is required, and who reviews it?Audit logging; retention
X7What are the trust boundaries and main threats?Threat Modeling (STRIDE)
X8How are secrets stored and rotated?Key Vault or equivalent; managed identities
X9Is anything exposed to the public internet?WAF; DDoS protection; rate limiting; bot defense

9. Reliability and failure handling

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
R1What happens when each dependency is slow, down, or returns garbage? What degraded behavior is acceptable?Graceful degradation; fallbacks; timeouts
R2Which components are single points of failure, and what is the blast radius if each one fails?Redundancy; isolation (bulkheads, cells)
R3Is single-region acceptable, or is multi-zone or multi-region needed? Active-active or active-passive?Topology; data replication; cost
R4How are partial failures in multi-step workflows resolved?Compensation; reconciliation jobs
R5How is the system protected from overload: bursts, retry storms, noisy tenants?Rate limiting; load shedding; backpressure; bulkheads
R6How are backups taken, and when was a restore last tested?Backup policy; restore drills
R7What is the disaster recovery plan, and who runs it?DR runbooks; failover automation

10. Operations and observability

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
O1How do we know the system is healthy? Which SLIs and SLOs?Dashboards; alerts; error budgets
O2What are the logging, metrics, and tracing standards? Is there an existing platform?OpenTelemetry setup; correlation IDs; see Monitoring vs Observability
O3Who is on call, what's the support model, and how are incidents detected and escalated?Alert routing; runbooks; incident process
O4Where is it deployed, and which environments exist?Hosting (containers, PaaS, serverless); pipeline design
O5How is a bad release rolled back, including its database changes?Expand/contract migrations; feature flags
O6How are configuration and feature flags managed per environment?Config service; flag tooling
O7What alerts and runbooks must exist at launch?Operational readiness checklist
O8After launch, how are changes requested, approved, and prioritized, and who owns the system?Change governance; ownership; backlog
O9How and when will the system be retired, and what happens to its data?Decommissioning plan; data export and retention

11. Constraints

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
C1How big is the team, and what are they experienced with?Technology choices the team can actually operate
C2Are platforms, languages, cloud providers, or enterprise standards mandated?Solution space
C3What's the budget to build and to run (CapEx/OpEx)? Is there a monthly cloud ceiling?Managed vs self-hosted; serverless vs reserved capacity
C4Are there licensing constraints (open source policy, commercial licenses)?Library and product selection
C5For each major capability: build, buy, or SaaS?Integration effort; vendor lock-in
C6How are teams organized, and who will own which parts?Service boundaries (Conway's law)
C7Which existing systems must be reused or replaced?Integration and migration scope
C8Which constraints are truly fixed, and which are negotiable?Where to push back; the real solution space

Part 2 · Design review (during design)

Run this once a candidate design exists, and again before sign-off. Each question tests the design against the Part 1 answers.

12. Decisions

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
A1What are the key architectural decisions? Is each recorded as an ADR with alternatives and trade-offs, and who signs off?Architecture Decision Records
A2Which decisions are hard or expensive to reverse, and have they had proportionate scrutiny?Where to spend analysis time (one-way vs two-way doors)
A3Can any decision be deferred until we know more?Last responsible moment; options kept open
A4Is each pattern or technology chosen because it fits the Part 1 drivers, or because it's familiar or fashionable?Traceability from characteristics to style

13. Structure and boundaries

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
M1What are the key components, and does each have one clear responsibility?Component view; ownership
M2Do service and module boundaries align with business domains (bounded contexts)?Service granularity; team ownership
M3Where is coupling unavoidable, and is it explicit (contracts, shared libraries, shared data)?Integration contracts; fitness functions that guard it
M4Could this start as a modular monolith and extract services later?Deployment complexity now vs later

14. Risk

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
K1What are the biggest technical risks and unknowns, and which spike or proof of concept retires each one early?Early prototypes; iteration plan
K2Walking each dependency through slow, down, and wrong: does the design behave as R1 requires?Timeouts; circuit breakers; validation at boundaries
K3Has the design been threat-modeled against its trust boundaries (X7)?Threat Modeling
K4Which risks are we explicitly accepting, and who has signed off on them?Risk register; ADR consequences

15. Validation

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
V1How will we prove the design meets its targets: load tests, chaos tests, fitness functions?Test strategy; see Load Testing Tools
V2Have stakeholders reviewed the design against real scenarios, not just diagrams?Scenario walkthroughs
V3Which assumptions are we making that nobody has confirmed, and how will we know if they're wrong?Assumption log; follow-ups
V4What business and technical metrics will we track after launch, and how will we gather user feedback?Post-launch success measures; feedback loop

16. Evolution and cost

#QuestionWhat the answer drives
E1Which parts are most likely to change over the next 2–3 years, and how often?Isolating volatility; modularity
E2Are future requirements already known (channels, markets, products), and how do we avoid over-engineering for them today?Extension points; avoiding premature generality
E3If replacing a legacy system: big bang or incremental?Strangler Fig; see Legacy Migration Approaches
E4How will we detect architectural drift over time?Architectural Fitness Functions
E5What's the total cost of ownership, including infrastructure, licensing, and operational headcount?Hosting model; build vs buy; staffing
E6What technical debt are we knowingly taking on, and what's the plan to pay it down?Debt register; roadmap
E7Will a new developer understand this system in a year?Documentation; diagrams; ADRs

Red flags in answers

  • "It must scale infinitely" or "100% uptime." No number. Ask what an hour of downtime actually costs.
  • "Everything is critical." No priorities. Force a top-three ranking of characteristics.
  • "Real-time" with no latency figure. Ask: sub-100 ms, a few seconds, or a few minutes?
  • "Same as the old system." The old system's behavior is a set of undocumented requirements; capture them.
  • Requirements stated as solutions ("we need Kafka", "it has to be microservices"). Ask what problem the solution is meant to solve.
  • "We'll handle security later." Compliance and data classification shape hosting and data design from the start.
